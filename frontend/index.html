firesync

Fire detections: NASA FIRMS (MODIS C6.1, VIIRS C2)

Is this fire season worse than usual here?

firesync shows how many fire detections an area had in each month since January 2003. It joins the MODIS record (2003 onward) and the VIIRS record (2012 onward) on a single VIIRS scale, so a change in the calendar is a change on the ground, not a change of satellite.

Method

The calendar counts satellite fire detections (hot spots), not individual fires. Only detections with nominal or high confidence over vegetation fires are kept.

One scale for two sensors

VIIRS sees many more hot spots than MODIS over the same area. To put both on the VIIRS scale, we compare MODIS Aqua and VIIRS S-NPP in the years both were flying (2012 to 2024). For every biome, month of the year and day or night overpass we compute a conversion factor, one per calibration year.

  • January 2003 to January 2012: estimated from MODIS Aqua × factor, shown with an uncertainty range.
  • February 2012 to October 2026: observed by VIIRS S-NPP.
  • November 2026 onward: observed by VIIRS NOAA-20.

Uncertainty

Each estimated month shows the median across the calibration years and a range from the 5th to the 95th percentile. In a leave-one-year-out test, the observed value fell inside this range in 68% to 80% of the months, depending on the biome.

How the alert is assessed

Normal, Attention and Critical describe recent detection activity compared with the usual level for that time of year. The alert compares the last 7 days with a seasonal reference drawn from the same dates, within 15 days, in other years.

The retrospective test evaluates weekly alerts from 2013 to 2024, then checks the following 14 days. A high-detection outcome means at least 10 VIIRS detections and a total above the seasonal 90th percentile. The comparison is a simple persistence rule: high activity this week continues into the next period. Both rules are compared at the same alert frequency.

Local-area results

The test groups detections into H3 resolution 4 cells. Results describe these test cells on average; they do not establish performance for each area drawn on the map. Final figures are pending selection and evaluation of rule v1.

The reported percentage will mean the share of Critical alerts followed by unusually high detections, alongside the difference from the persistence rule. It is not the share of all high-detection periods caught by the alert.

Whole-biome results

Local-area performance does not carry over to a whole biome. Biome results will report how often each alert level occurs, without a predictive success percentage. Final frequencies are pending the evaluation of rule v1.

Historical example: Pantanal, 2020

The Pantanal case compares alert levels with the date of the Mato Grosso do Sul emergency decree (Decree 080/2020, official state source). The final lead time will be reported with the selected rule. An alert before a decree indicates elevated activity before the official response; it does not show that the alert predicted a fire before it started. This case is an illustration, not statistical evidence.

Limits

The alert and its test outcome use the same VIIRS sensor. The test measures subsequent satellite detections, not burned area. Comparison with NASA MCD64A1 burned-area data remains a next step. The seasonal reference excludes the evaluated year but includes later years, so this is a retrospective assessment, not a historical forecast using only information available at the time.

firesync describes the past and the present; it does not forecast fire. Months with a satellite outage are marked as missing data, which is different from a month with zero detections.
